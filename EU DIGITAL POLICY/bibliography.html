A note on method

What a citation here means. Each note in the brief records not only which source is attached to a claim but what that source actually does for it: whether it states the proposition, supports it in part — establishing a narrower case, or the components a figure is computed from — or is only context, which is not a citation at all. A URL alone was never treated as evidence.

Where a source is missing. Some works the brief refers to could not be pinned to a specific publication during the source pass. Those entries appear with no public URL located rather than being quietly dropped or replaced with a loosely related substitute.

Verification dates. Every claim carries a verification date of its own, and every one of them currently reads the same day — the day the dataset was compiled. The field is per-record so that claims can be re-checked individually, but no claim has yet been re-checked on a different day from any other, and the reader should read these dates as one compilation date rather than as evidence of independent checking. Negative claims — statements that something has not happened — are true only at an instant, and that instant is the compilation date for all of them.

How each claim is graded. A single "requires verification" flag was doing five jobs at once, and it made an argument — which no citation can ever settle — look like a fact still waiting for a source. Every claim is now graded on what actually carries it. The grade is derived from the claim's type and its sources on every load, never stored, so it cannot drift away from the evidence it describes.
